1. PATIENT CASE SCENARIO
------------------------
An 18-year-old male with a known seizure disorder (controlled on regular 
levetiracetam for 3 years) was administered a loading dose of Phenytoin 
following a breakthrough seizure episode. Shortly after initiation, the patient 
presented with an acute fever (>38°C) accompanied by a sudden, generalized 
erythematous rash covered with numerous non-follicular sterile pustules, 
alongside cerebellar signs. Clinical and dermatological evaluation confirmed 
the diagnosis of Acute Generalized Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP).

2. MEDICATION HISTORY & CLINICAL TIMELINE
-----------------------------------------
* Suspected Drug: Phenytoin (Loading dose, Systemic)
* Concomitant Medication: Levetiracetam (taken regularly for 3 years without prior reaction)
* Action Taken (Dechallenge): Phenytoin was immediately discontinued. The patient 
  was treated with tapering oral corticosteroids (40 mg) and antihistamines.
* Outcome: Complete clinical resolution with skin desquamation occurred within 
  15 days (Positive Dechallenge).
* Rechallenge: Not attempted due to the severity of the cutaneous reaction.

3. PHARMACOVIGILANCE ANALYSIS (WHO-UMC CAUSALITY ASSESSMENT)
------------------------------------------------------------
* Temporal Relationship: The onset of fever and sterile pustular eruption 
  occurred directly after the administration of Phenytoin.
* Exclusion of Alternative Causes: Levetiracetam had been tolerated safely 
  for 3 years, ruling it out as the acute trigger; infectious and pre-existing 
  pustular dermatoses were excluded.
* Dechallenge: Withdrawal of Phenytoin and supportive steroid therapy resulted 
  in complete recovery within 15 days.
* Rechallenge: Ethically contraindicated for Severe Cutaneous Adverse 
  Reactions (SCARs).

4. FINAL IDENTIFICATION
-----------------------
* Suspected Drug: Phenytoin
* Identified ADR: Acute Generalized Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP)
* Causality Category: PROBABLE / LIKELY
